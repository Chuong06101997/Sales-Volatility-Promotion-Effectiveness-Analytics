# Risk Register

**Project:** Sales Volatility, Promotion Effectiveness & Forecasting Benchmark Review

This document tracks what could change after this project's release that would weaken or invalidate its findings. The Decision Log covers choices already made. The Assumption Register covers what those choices rely on. This document covers what happens next — the events, changes, or gaps that would require someone to revisit the work.

## Heat Map Summary

12 of the risks above are analytical or forecast-related and carried over from the original project scope; 2 (R13, R14) are new and cover whether the business side actually follows through on what this project recommends.

- **High overall risk:** R01, R02, R05, R06, R08, R10 — six risks, mostly tied to generalizing beyond the two stores studied, or to promotion and business conditions shifting.
- **Moderate overall risk:** R03, R04, R07, R09, R12, R13, R14 — seven risks, mostly about forecast drift over time and whether recommended next steps actually get resourced.
- **Low overall risk:** R11 — one risk, and it stays low because weather already showed no measurable effect on sales.
- **Actively monitored:** R02, R03, R06, R07, R08, R09, R11 have a defined early warning signal someone is expected to watch for.
- **Accepted rather than actively managed:** R10 (unmeasured external factors are a limit of any dataset like this one, not something a mitigation plan fixes) and R12 (business priorities changing isn't a project risk to control, just something to track separately from the statistics).

## Governance Perspective

The Decision Log records what was decided during the analysis — which test to run, which baseline to build, what to exclude. The Assumption Register records what has to stay true for those decisions to still make sense — that the data stays similarly clean, that the two stores stay representative of themselves, that promotions keep behaving the way they did in 2021-2023. This Risk Register is the next step down that chain: it's what happens if one of those assumptions stops holding.

Read together, the three documents answer three different questions a reviewer would ask. What did you do, and why? What does that depend on? And what would tell us it's time to redo it? Keeping them separate instead of folding everything into one document makes it easier to update just the piece that actually changed, instead of rewriting the whole project history every time something shifts.
